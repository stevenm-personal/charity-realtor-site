# SEO guide

This document is the authoritative SEO reference for future development of the Charity Menefee website. Use it alongside `AGENTS.md` and `BRAND.md`. Community-page work must also follow `COMMUNITY_PAGES.md`.

SEO requirements do not override the site's editorial voice, Fair Housing safeguards, factual-accuracy standards, accessibility requirements, or Kansas Dusk design direction.

## Primary SEO goal

The primary SEO objective of this website is to generate qualified local real estate leads for Charity Menefee by increasing her visibility when people in Wichita, Kansas, and the surrounding communities she genuinely serves are searching for a Realtor, real estate agent, or help buying or selling real estate.

Important local search intent includes natural variations of searches such as:

- Realtor in Wichita KS.
- Realtors in Wichita KS.
- Wichita Realtor.
- Wichita real estate agent.
- Realtor in Valley Center KS.
- Valley Center Realtor.
- Realtor in Derby KS.
- Realtor in Maize KS.
- Realtor in Andover KS.

Relevant service-related searches may also include intent around:

- Buying a home in the Wichita area.
- Selling a home in the Wichita area.
- Finding a listing agent.
- Relocating within the Wichita area.
- Land and acreage around Wichita.
- New construction in communities Charity serves.

These examples represent search intent. They are not exact phrases that should be mechanically repeated throughout the site.

The goal is not simply to maximize rankings or traffic. The desired outcome is:

1. Relevant local search visibility.
2. A qualified visitor.
3. A useful, trustworthy website experience.
4. The visitor contacts Charity.

A lower-volume search that produces a legitimate prospective client may be more valuable than a high-volume search that attracts visitors with little likelihood of needing Charity's services.

## Local search strategy

Treat the website and Charity's Google Business Profile as complementary parts of the broader local-search strategy.

Website SEO should help establish:

- What Charity does.
- Where she works.
- Her local knowledge.
- The services she provides.
- Why the individual page is useful for the visitor's particular search.

Community pages should be capable of ranking independently for relevant searches about their communities. Do not assume the homepage must rank for every city-specific query.

For example, it is a successful outcome if a search for a Valley Center Realtor leads directly to `/communities/valley-center/`, provided that the page satisfies the visitor's search intent and offers a clear path to contact Charity.

## Ranking philosophy

High rankings are desirable, particularly for high-intent local searches, but rankings are a means to the business goal rather than an end in themselves.

Prioritize:

1. Qualified local visibility.
2. Relevance to the searcher's intent.
3. Genuinely useful and original local content.
4. Trust and accuracy.
5. Conversion into legitimate inquiries.
6. Sustainable long-term search visibility.

Do not pursue rankings through:

- Keyword stuffing.
- City-name swapping.
- Thin location pages.
- Mass-produced AI content.
- Misleading titles.
- Irrelevant traffic acquisition.
- Content created primarily for search engines.

## SEO philosophy

Optimize for real people first and search engines second.

SEO should come primarily from:

- Genuinely useful local content.
- Clear site structure.
- Descriptive page titles and headings.
- Intentional internal linking.
- Fast performance.
- Accessible, semantic markup.
- Accurate local and business information.
- Technically clean pages.

Avoid:

- Keyword stuffing.
- Doorway pages.
- Thin location pages.
- Repetitive city-name swapping.
- Fake FAQs.
- Fabricated local facts.
- Excessive copy added only to increase word count.
- Writing for SEO scores instead of visitors.
- Publishing pages merely to target another keyword variation.

## Local SEO

The primary geographic focus is Wichita, Kansas, and surrounding communities.

Community pages should identify their actual city naturally through:

- The page title.
- The H1.
- Introductory copy.
- Genuinely local landmarks, roads, housing context, and development information.
- Relevant internal links.
- Image alt text when the location is naturally part of what the image shows.

Do not repeatedly force exact-match phrases such as "Realtor in Wichita KS," "best Realtor in Valley Center," or similar variations into copy. The site should sound natural and useful.

Community pages must follow `COMMUNITY_PAGES.md`. Do not create a community page until there is enough useful, verified material to justify it. Every community guide should contain enough real specificity that replacing the city name with another city would make the content incorrect.

## Search intent and page purpose

Before creating or substantially revising a page, identify the useful visitor need it serves. A page should have a clear purpose that is not already satisfied by another page.

- Service pages should answer practical questions about buying, selling, or land and acreage.
- Community guides should provide broad, evergreen orientation and local property context.
- Blog posts should provide deeper, narrower, or more time-sensitive information.
- Contact and About pages should support trust and communication without being inflated with search-focused copy.

Do not create near-duplicate pages for small keyword variations.

## Page titles

Every indexable page should eventually have a unique, useful HTML title.

Titles should:

- Describe the actual page.
- Include a location naturally when it is relevant.
- Include Charity Menefee when that adds identification or context.
- Avoid repetitive keyword strings.
- Remain reasonably concise.

Style examples, not mandatory formulas:

- `Wichita, KS Real Estate Guide | Charity Menefee`
- `Valley Center, KS Community Guide | Charity Menefee`
- `Buying a Home in the Wichita Area | Charity Menefee`

Do not mechanically apply one title formula when a clearer title fits the page better.

## Meta descriptions

Each important indexable page should eventually have a unique meta description.

Meta descriptions should:

- Summarize the page accurately.
- Sound conversational.
- Encourage a useful click without hype or artificial urgency.
- Avoid stuffing keyword variants.
- Generally remain concise enough for search-result display.

Do not fabricate or imply unverified claims such as top Realtor, best Realtor, number-one agent, or market leader.

## Headings

- Give each page one clear H1.
- Use H2 and H3 levels according to the actual content hierarchy.
- Make headings useful for visitors scanning the page.
- Do not create headings solely to insert keywords.
- Do not skip heading levels for visual styling. Use CSS for appearance.

## URLs

Keep URLs:

- Short.
- Descriptive.
- Lowercase.
- Human-readable.
- Stable.

Preferred patterns include:

- `/services/buying/`
- `/communities/wichita/`

Avoid unnecessary date folders, parameters, duplicate paths, and keyword-heavy URLs. Consider redirects and existing inbound links before changing any published URL.

## Community pages

Community pages are evergreen local guides, not thin SEO landing pages. Follow `COMMUNITY_PAGES.md` for their editorial structure, locality standard, factual verification, photography, Fair Housing review, and quality test.

Do not create community pages in bulk from a template with only the city name changed. Shared components and a recognizable page structure are appropriate, but the useful substance must be specific to each community.

## Blog strategy

Blog content should support, not duplicate, evergreen service and community pages.

Use blog posts for:

- Deeper neighborhood profiles.
- Individual subdivisions.
- New-development updates.
- Local events or meaningful changes.
- Practical buying and selling education.
- Other narrower or more time-sensitive information.

When useful, link blog articles to relevant evergreen community or service pages. Community and service pages may link to related articles that genuinely add depth.

Avoid publishing large volumes of thin, repetitive, or lightly edited AI-generated articles. Quality is more important than quantity.

## Internal linking

Create intentional relationships between pages, such as:

1. Homepage to Services to Buying, Selling, or Land and Acreage.
2. Homepage to Communities to individual community guides.
3. Community guide to a relevant deeper blog article.
4. Blog article to a relevant community or service page.

Use descriptive anchor text when it reads naturally. Avoid vague anchors when a clearer label would help, but do not over-optimize every link. Avoid excessive, repeated links to the same destination within one page.

Internal links should help visitors understand where to go next, not merely distribute keywords.

## Images and alt text

Continue following the image organization and responsive Astro optimization rules in `AGENTS.md`.

Use meaningful alt text for informative images. Alt text should:

- Describe what is actually visible.
- Mention the location when it is naturally relevant.
- Remain concise.
- Support the purpose of the image in its page context.

Do not stuff keywords into alt text. Decorative images should use appropriate empty alt text or another accessible decorative treatment instead of unnecessary SEO-focused descriptions.

Preserve responsive sizing and optimized formats so imagery supports performance rather than undermining it.

## Structured data

Before launch, evaluate structured data that accurately represents the verified business and site configuration.

Potentially relevant schema types include:

- `Person`
- `RealEstateAgent`
- `LocalBusiness` or an appropriate subtype
- `WebSite`
- `BreadcrumbList`
- `Article` or `BlogPosting` for published articles

Use only schema types and properties that fit the visible content and actual business arrangement. Do not add structured data containing unverified:

- Addresses.
- Phone numbers.
- Ratings or reviews.
- Awards.
- Service areas.
- Credentials.
- Brokerage or license information.

Structured data must match information visible to visitors. Never use schema to make claims the page does not make.

**Status (2026-08-19):** implemented — a site-wide `Person` entity (name, url, jobTitle, telephone, email, `worksFor` → Bricktown ICT Realty) on every page, and `BlogPosting` (headline, description, author, datePublished, image, canonical URL) on the current article. No `RealEstateAgent`/`LocalBusiness` type is used, since that would imply an address this project doesn't have and want to invent. `WebSite` and `BreadcrumbList` were deliberately left out: the site has no visible breadcrumb UI for `BreadcrumbList` to accurately reflect, and `WebSite` wouldn't add anything `Person.url` doesn't already establish.

## Business information and NAP consistency

Keep Charity's name, brokerage affiliation, phone number, email address, office information, and other verified business details consistent everywhere they appear.

Do not invent missing contact or business information. Before launch, confirm the exact business information, brokerage requirements, and any required licensing or disclosure presentation.

When address information becomes available, decide carefully whether it is an appropriate public business address before treating it as NAP data.

## Canonical URLs

Before production launch, every indexable page should have an appropriate canonical URL based on:

`https://charitymenefee.com/`

Canonical paths should match the preferred production URL for each page. Do not point production canonical tags to a `workers.dev` preview URL.

Do not add production canonical behavior prematurely if the site's final domain or launch state has not been confirmed.

**Status (2026-08-19):** implemented. `astro.config.mjs` sets `site: 'https://charitymenefee.com'`, and `BaseLayout.astro` emits one self-referencing `<link rel="canonical">` per page (and a matching `og:url`), derived from that config value plus the page's own path. This is safe alongside the current global `noindex` — a canonical tag has no effect on indexing eligibility by itself.

## Sitemap

Before launch:

- Generate an XML sitemap.
- Verify that it resolves correctly on the production domain.
- Include intended indexable pages.
- Exclude pages that should not be indexed.
- Check that canonical URLs and sitemap URLs agree.
- Do not include preview-only or development URLs.

**Status (2026-08-19):** implemented via the official `@astrojs/sitemap` integration. Output is `sitemap-index.xml` + `sitemap-0.xml` in `dist/`, using the production domain. Privacy, Disclaimer, and 404 are excluded via the integration's `filter`. The sitemap's presence in the build doesn't make the site indexable — that's still controlled by `noindex` below.

## Robots and indexing

Preserve the current development `noindex` behavior during ordinary development work. Do not remove `noindex` merely because SEO improvements are being made.

Before production launch, perform a deliberate indexing review:

1. Confirm the production domain.
2. Confirm which pages should be indexable.
3. Remove or revise development-only `noindex` controls.
4. Verify page-level robots directives.
5. Verify `robots.txt` behavior if one is used.
6. Verify the XML sitemap.
7. Verify canonical URLs.
8. Confirm that preview and production environments do not send conflicting signals.

Removing `noindex` is an explicit launch action, not a routine side effect of another task.

**Status (2026-08-19):** `public/robots.txt` is already production-ready (`Allow: /`, references the production sitemap) rather than blocking crawlers outright. That's deliberate: a robots.txt `Disallow` would stop Google from ever crawling a page far enough to see its `noindex` meta tag, which is the actual protection during development. `Privacy` and `Disclaimer` are *not* separately disallowed in robots.txt for the same reason — they rely on their own permanent `robots="noindex, follow"` page directive, which requires them to stay crawlable to be honored. This means launch now reduces to one deliberate step: **remove the temporary global `noindex, nofollow` default in `BaseLayout.astro`.** `404.astro` already passes its own literal `robots="noindex, nofollow"` independent of that default, and Privacy/Disclaimer already carry their own permanent `noindex, follow` — neither needs to change at launch.

## Performance and Core Web Vitals

Preserve the site's static-first Astro architecture and lightweight JavaScript approach.

SEO work should not unnecessarily add:

- Heavy client-side JavaScript.
- Large dependencies.
- Unnecessary third-party scripts.
- Oversized or poorly sized images.
- Render-blocking resources.
- Layout shifts caused by missing image dimensions or unstable content.

Continue using Astro's responsive image pipeline for large photography. Treat loading performance, visual stability, responsiveness, and interaction quality as part of SEO and visitor experience.

## Accessibility

SEO work must preserve or improve:

- Semantic HTML.
- Logical, useful headings.
- Keyboard navigation.
- Visible focus states.
- Proper form and control labels.
- Meaningful image alt text.
- Adequate color contrast.
- Reduced-motion support.

Do not sacrifice accessibility for keyword placement, visual design, or SEO tooling scores.

## Factual accuracy and freshness

Prefer evergreen information on core service and community pages.

Verify time-sensitive facts before publication, including:

- Home prices.
- Inventory.
- Development status.
- Builders.
- School boundaries.
- Municipal projects.
- Taxes.
- Market statistics.
- Current amenities or transportation changes.

Do not write current claims from memory or assumptions. Use authoritative primary sources when practical. Time-sensitive material may be better suited to dated blog content than permanent community-page copy.

Continue following the broader factual-integrity and Fair Housing rules in `AGENTS.md` and the community-specific verification rules in `COMMUNITY_PAGES.md`.

## SEO quality test

Before considering a major page complete, ask:

1. What visitor search intent does this page actually satisfy?
2. Is the HTML title unique and descriptive?
3. Is there one clear H1?
4. Is the page genuinely useful without considering search engines?
5. Is the local information specific and accurate?
6. Does the page unnecessarily duplicate another page?
7. Are there natural internal-link opportunities?
8. Is the page fast, stable, responsive, and accessible?
9. Is any wording present only because someone thought it might improve rankings?
10. Would the page still be worth publishing if Google did not exist?

If the answer to question 10 is no, the page likely needs better content or may not justify its existence.

## Development and production launch

Keep the two environments conceptually separate.

### Development

- Uses the `workers.dev` preview URL.
- Preserves `noindex` and `nofollow` controls.
- Privacy and Disclaimer contain working draft content, pending final human/brokerage review before launch.
- Publishes Blog articles only when genuinely useful, accurate material is ready.
- Google Analytics 4 is implemented and hostname-gated to `charitymenefee.com` (see `README.md`), so it does not load on the `workers.dev` preview or in local development.
- Uses the temporary contact-form delivery configuration documented in `README.md`.
- As of 2026-08-19, deliberately *does* emit production-domain canonical URLs, Open Graph metadata, a sitemap, and structured data (see the status notes above) — this was an authorized SEO-preparation task, not premature launch. Indexing itself stays blocked by the global `noindex, nofollow` default, not by domain ambiguity.
- Should not be submitted for indexing.

### Production

- Uses `https://charitymenefee.com/`.
- Exposes only pages ready for public use and indexing.
- Uses verified canonical URLs.
- Provides a verified sitemap and appropriate robots behavior.
- Uses only accurate structured data.
- Displays consistent, verified business and contact information.
- Displays Privacy and Disclaimer content that has completed final human/brokerage review.
- Uses the approved production contact-form destination and configuration documented in `README.md`.
- Documents any selected analytics accurately in the Privacy Policy.

Do not automatically enable production SEO behavior during routine development tasks. Treat the transition as a dedicated technical SEO and launch pass.
